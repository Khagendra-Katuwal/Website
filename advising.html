Student Mentoring

I have had the opportunity to co-advise seven undergraduate and graduate students from academic institutions in the United States, Nepal, India, and Bangladesh. Their research projects focus on solar physics, heliophysics, solar magnetic activity, and space weather, using observational data and computational methods.

  1. Cutter Beck
    Institution: Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI), USA
    Education: Bachelor's degree, graduated in 2025
    Research Project: Improving Automated Coronal Hole Detection Using Semi-Automatic Methods

    Cutter's undergraduate research focused on improving the identification of solar coronal holes using automated and semi-automated detection methods. His project involved computational approaches to analyzing solar observations, contributing to the development and evaluation of coronal-hole detection techniques.

  2. Bhairav Ale
    Institution: Central Department of Physics, Tribhuvan University, Nepal
    Education: M.Sc. in Physics, graduated in 2025
    Research Project: Tracking the Evolution of Solar Coronal Holes

    Bhairav investigated the temporal evolution of solar coronal holes using observations of the solar atmosphere. His project focused on tracking changes in coronal-hole structure, morphology, and spatial distribution over time. His research interests include solar physics and space weather.

  3. Akash Vinod Shirke
    Institution: UPES, Dehradun, India
    Education: Master's degree, graduated in 2025
    Research Projects: Distribution of Coronal Holes During Solar Cycle 24 Behavior of Solar Jets in Active Regions, Quiet-Sun Regions, and Coronal Holes

    Akash worked on two solar-physics research projects. The first examined the spatial distribution of solar coronal holes during Solar Cycle 24, while the second investigated solar-jet behavior in different solar environments, including active regions, quiet-Sun regions, and coronal holes. His research interests include solar astrophysics, space weather, and computational astrophysics.

  4. Ashesh Pokharel
    Education: M.Sc. in Physics, graduated in 2024
    Research Project: Tracking Solar Coronal Holes During Solar Cycle 24

    Ashesh studied the evolution of solar coronal holes throughout Solar Cycle 24. His research focused on identifying and tracking coronal holes over time to investigate changes in their occurrence and spatial distribution. His interests include heliophysics and solar atmospheric dynamics.

  5. Dharanesh Palaniappan
    Institution: Madras Christian College, India
    Research Projects: Evolution of Solar Active Regions Merging of Solar Coronal Holes

    Dharanesh has worked on the evolution of solar active regions and the merging of coronal holes. His projects use observational data to investigate changes in solar magnetic structures and coronal-hole morphology. His research interests include observational and data-driven heliophysics.

  6. Sujan Dahal
    Education: M.Sc. in Physics — thesis in progress, Nepal
    Research Project: Relationship Between Coronal-Hole Properties and In Situ Solar-Wind Parameters

    Sujan's research investigates relationships between the observed properties of solar coronal holes and physical parameters measured in situ in the solar wind. His project aims to better understand the connection between coronal-hole characteristics and solar-wind conditions in interplanetary space.

  7. Asif Shahriar
    Institution: Jashore University of Science and Technology, Bangladesh
    Education: Undergraduate student — currently pursuing his degree
    Research Project: Splitting of Solar Coronal Holes

    Asif is conducting undergraduate research on the splitting of solar coronal holes. His project examines how coronal-hole structures evolve and separate into distinct regions using solar observations, with an emphasis on their morphological evolution and changes in coronal-hole boundaries.
